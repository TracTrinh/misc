---
prompt: Do people rely too much on phones? — "Yes, phones are everywhere."
hint: Nêu lập trường vừa phải, lý do, thực tế xã hội, chốt cân bằng.
model: Yes, to some extent. Phones are useful, but they can make people check information and messages too automatically. In daily life, many people reach for their phones during meals, meetings, or short moments of boredom. The issue is not the device itself, but whether people can still control their attention.
---
prompt: Should children learn art at school? — "Yes, art is good."
hint: Art giúp gì ngoài giải trí? Thực tế trường học?
model: Yes, I think art should remain part of education. It helps children express ideas in ways that are not only academic or exam-based. In many schools, students spend most of their time on measurable subjects, so art can provide balance. It should not replace core subjects, but it gives children a different form of development.
---
prompt: Why do people travel abroad? — "Because they like travel."
hint: Nguyên nhân sâu hơn: experience, status, escape, learning.
model: People travel abroad for several reasons. Some want to experience different food, languages, and ways of living, while others simply want a break from routine. International travel has also become easier to see online, so people often feel curious about places they have seen on social media. At its best, travel can make people more open-minded, not just more entertained.
---
prompt: Will online shopping become more popular? — "Yes, it is convenient."
hint: Dự đoán có điều kiện, ví dụ xã hội, giới hạn.
model: Yes, it will probably keep growing because it saves time and offers more choice. Many people are already comfortable buying clothes, electronics, and even groceries through apps. However, physical shops will not disappear completely because people still like trying products and having social experiences. The future is likely to be a mix of both.
---
prompt: How can cities reduce pollution? — "Use more public transport."
hint: Giải pháp nhiều tầng: transport, rules, habits.
model: Cities can reduce pollution by improving public transport, controlling industrial emissions, and making walking or cycling safer. Transport is important because daily commuting creates a lot of pollution in crowded areas. However, rules alone are not enough if people do not have practical alternatives. A good solution must make cleaner choices affordable and convenient.
---
prompt: Are old people and young people different in how they use technology? — "Yes, young people use it more."
hint: So sánh theo mục đích và mức độ thoải mái.
model: Yes, there is usually a clear difference. Younger people often use technology for socialising, entertainment, study, and work at the same time, so they move between apps very quickly. Older people may use fewer functions but often use them for practical needs such as calling family or paying bills. The difference is not intelligence, but habit and exposure.
---
prompt: What are the disadvantages of fast food? — "It is unhealthy."
hint: Không chỉ health, còn money, habits, lifestyle.
model: The main disadvantage is health, especially if people eat fast food too often. It can also shape people's habits because they become used to quick, salty, or sugary meals instead of cooking. In busy cities, fast food is attractive because it is cheap and convenient, but that convenience can hide long-term costs. The problem is frequency, not eating it once in a while.
---
prompt: Should museums be free? — "Yes, everyone can visit."
hint: Cân bằng access và cost.
model: I think many museums should be free or at least affordable. They protect culture and education, so price should not stop ordinary families or students from visiting. At the same time, museums need money for staff, maintenance, and exhibitions. A balanced approach could be free entry for basic collections and paid tickets for special events.
